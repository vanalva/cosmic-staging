          Quick answers

          Popular problems

          The short answer first, the full guide when you need it.

          Why is my paint peeling?

          Almost always moisture or prep. Paint lets go when water gets behind it (leaks, humidity, wet wood), when it went on over dirt, chalk or gloss, or when latex went straight over old oil paint. Fix the cause first, then scrape off everything loose, sand the edges smooth, spot-prime the bare areas and repaint.

          Read full guide

          Do I need primer?

          Usually yes when the surface is new, patched, glossy, stained or bare, on cabinets, and for a big color change. You can often skip it when you repaint a clean, sound, previously painted wall in a similar color.

          Read full guideThe full answer

          How many gallons of paint do I need?

          Add up the wall area (width × height of each wall), take off about 20 sq ft per door and 15 sq ft per window, then divide by 350–400 sq ft per gallon for each coat. Plan on two coats: a 12 × 12 ft bedroom with 8 ft ceilings usually needs about 2 gallons. Rough or porous surfaces take more; check the coverage on the can.

          Read full guideUse the Paint Calculator

          Can I paint over glossy paint?

          Yes, once it is dull and clean. Wash it with a degreaser, scuff-sand with 180–220 grit until the shine is gone (or use a liquid deglosser), wipe off the dust, then use a bonding primer. If the old paint is oil-based, the bonding primer is not optional: latex straight over oil peels.

          Read full guide

          What roller nap should I use?

          Match the nap to the surface: 1/4 in. or foam for very smooth things like doors and cabinets, 3/8 in. for most interior walls and ceilings, 1/2 in. for light texture, and 3/4 in. or more for stucco, brick and block. Too long a nap on a smooth wall leaves an orange-peel texture; too short on a rough one misses the low spots.

          Read full guide

          Why can I see roller marks?

          Usually from rolling over paint that has started to dry, too little paint on the roller, or pressing too hard. Keep a wet edge: work in sections about 3–4 ft wide, load the roller fully, and finish each section with light passes in one direction. Once it is dry, sand the ridges lightly and roll one more coat.

          Read full guide

          How long does paint need to dry?

          Most latex wall paint is dry to the touch in about an hour and ready for a second coat in 2–4 hours. Oil-based paint takes 6–8 hours to the touch and about 24 hours to recoat. Cool or humid weather stretches every number, and full hardness takes 2–4 weeks, so go easy on fresh cabinets and trim. Check the can for your paint's times.

          Read full guide

          Why is my paint bubbling?

          Blisters mean the paint lost its grip as it dried: heat (painting in direct sun or on a hot surface), moisture behind the paint, or a dirty or damp surface. Let it dry, scrape the blisters open, sand smooth, fix any moisture source, prime and repaint. Tiny bubbles while you roll are different: they come from overworking the paint or a cheap cover, and most pop on their own.

          Read full guide

          Can I paint exterior surfaces after rain?

          Wait until the surface is dry all the way through, not just to the touch: at least a day after rain in sun and wind, longer for wood in shade or humid weather. Then check the forecast: most exterior paints need several dry hours after you finish and a minimum temperature, both printed on the can. In Austin summers, paint the shady side and skip the hottest hours.

          Read full guide

          What happens if I skip sanding?

          On a flat, clean, previously painted wall you can often skip it. On gloss, cabinets, trim and anything with bumps, drips or old brush marks you cannot: new paint will not grip shiny surfaces, and it shows every flaw underneath. A quick scuff with a fine sanding sponge takes minutes and decides whether the job lasts.
